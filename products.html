The two products, and where each one stops.

64Griddle and PathWay AI are the products 64ARCS has built for itself. Both are early pilots that open in a browser, with nothing to install and no sign-in. Neither is finished, and each has a panel listing where it stops.

64Griddle

Pilot

It reads your prompt before you send it and tells you what is missing.

Browser pilot v0.5.3; guidance reviewed 8 September 2026.

You write a request to an AI assistant in the draft box. As you type, local rules mark vague requirements, missing task details, repetition and conflicting instructions in the draft. Building, debugging, research, writing, summaries and data analysis each get their own checks, and you can correct the task type the pilot detects. Each suggestion shows what triggered it and the principle it rests on.

You apply a suggestion or leave it, compare the original with the proposed text, edit the preview, then copy it or replace your draft. Nothing is rewritten behind your back, and the words that end up in the box are yours. Eight example drafts, from a web app build to a simple question, are there to try before you paste your own.

An extension for Chrome and Edge and a macOS companion are part of a pilot by arrangement.

Where this pilot stops

  • It reads only the current textbox. It cannot see earlier messages or read attachments.
  • It uses local rules, not a model. It cannot guarantee a better answer.
  • It never invents names, deadlines, technologies or requirements — it only points at gaps.
  • These checks have not been tested for whether they improve a model’s output.
  • Token counts are approximate.
  • Editors for live AI providers are not certified yet.

Your prompt stays on your device — the pilot makes no network requests. What the extension stores, and what host sites still see.

PathWay AI

Pilot

A place for a student to work, inside the boundary their teacher set.

A teacher sets the terms for each project separately, choosing which kinds of help are allowed. Inside those terms the student writes their own draft and can ask for help to understand the task, plan the work in steps, practise an idea it depends on, or review small corrections to their own saved writing. When a request crosses the boundary, the reply says why and offers a permitted next step. The pilot carries four practice projects, in English, History, Geography and Science, each with a fixed reading list.

Practice questions are written in advance; the browser pilot serves only questions a teacher has reviewed, and none are reviewed yet.

Saved drafts, help requests, the replies shown, writing reviews and permission changes go into an activity record that the student and the teacher preview both see. Unsaved typing is not recorded, and the record makes no judgement about who wrote the work.

Where this pilot stops

  • Replies are prepared examples. There is no connected generative model in this preview.
  • It is not a personalised tutor and cannot answer personal follow-up questions.
  • It is not an authorship detector. The activity record is not a verdict.
  • Teacher controls are a demonstration. School accounts are not connected.
  • It does not search the web or verify a student’s claims.
  • It cannot produce a finished piece of work.

Work stays in your browser by default. Sharing practice requests is optional and off unless you turn it on. What that sends, and what it does not.
