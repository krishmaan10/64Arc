Products

Two products in pilot. A pilot is a working version used by a small group under agreed terms, with its limits written down here rather than discovered later. Neither is a public release.

64Griddle

Pilot Browser extension · Mac companion

Reviews an AI prompt in the field where you write it, before you send it. 64Griddle watches the text field you are typing in, points out what an agent would need that the draft does not say — an outcome, context, a deliverable — and offers wording you accept or reject, one change at a time. You stay the author. Sending is still your action, in the original app.

What it does

  • Reads the focused prompt field on the sites you allow, and nothing else on the page.
  • Finds concrete gaps: a missing outcome, missing context, an unstated deliverable, a duplicated instruction.
  • Shows each suggestion with its reasoning. You supply the facts it asks for, then accept or reject.
  • Runs as deterministic checks on your machine. No prompt text leaves it.

What it does not do

  • Rewrite your prompt for you, or send it.
  • Use a language model. The checks are rules, and they are English-first.
  • Claim better answers. Whether a reviewed prompt gets a better result is what a pilot measures.

Where it stands

  • Extension and workspace 0.5.3; Mac companion 0.5.4.
  • Compatibility with each site’s composer is verified site by site during a pilot.
  • By arrangement. Not in any store.

PathWay AI

Pilot For schools · Students and teachers

A learning assistant a school can hand a student, knowing what it will and will not do. It helps with the work and cannot do the work: it explains what an assignment asks, plans the steps, asks questions to check understanding, corrects writing the student has already done, and finds sources. The teacher sets the terms per assignment and reads a plain record of how the work was made.

What it does

  • Six kinds of help, each a named mode. The teacher decides which ones an assignment allows.
  • Reads every request with fixed rules and a small classifier trained on how students ask for help and for shortcuts. The same request gets the same answer, however it is worded or how often it is repeated.
  • Records every request, what it was taken for, and every decision, in sentences a teacher can read. Across a session it notes what a teacher would notice, with each threshold stated.
  • Points a student to a person when a message is about them rather than the work.

What it does not do

  • Write the essay, continue a paragraph, or give the answer to a set question, in any wording.
  • Score writing as AI-generated, or call anyone a cheat. The record describes; the teacher decides.
  • Prove who wrote text pasted in from elsewhere. It shows the text arriving instead of being written.

Where it stands

  • Working boundary engine, teacher record and student interface, under 46 automated checks.
  • The model connection, accounts and storage are set up with each school for its pilot.
  • By arrangement with a school. Not a public release.
