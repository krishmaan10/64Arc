01 — Understand

Map the business as it is.

Before any structure can be decided, the business has to be described accurately — including the parts that were never written down. This is where the “before” picture above comes from.

What we map

People and roles. Processes as they are actually performed. Customers, dealers and partners. Existing systems and what each is really used for. Where information originates, where it stops, and who carries it across the gap.

Where we look

On the floor and in the field, not only in meeting rooms. The spreadsheet someone maintains privately usually explains more about a business than its ERP does.

What comes out

A process map, a systems inventory, an information-flow diagram and a ranked list of bottlenecks — each traced to a business cost.

We map what people do, not what the org chart says they do.
The workaround is usually the requirement.

02 — Architect

Decide the structure.

What stays, what changes, what connects, what gets built, and where AI belongs. This is the decision the rest of the engagement depends on.

The business model

One definition of a customer, a product, a price, an order and a stock position — agreed across departments that currently define them differently.

The platform map

Which screens exist, who uses each one, and what each is allowed to see.

The keep / replace / build decision

Made explicitly, per system, with the reasoning recorded — so nobody has to re-litigate it eighteen months later.

Where intelligence belongs

The specific decisions where AI with the right context changes an outcome, and the ones where it would just be decoration.

The structure is agreed with the business before it is built.
If it cannot be explained to the people who run the company, it is not finished.

03 — Build

Build what the structure calls for.

Built in sequence, released in pieces a branch can use, and always on the shared foundation — never as separate products that will need joining later.

Foundation first

The shared business model, the data layer, permissions and the rules are built before the screens that depend on them.

Released in working slices

Each release does a real job for a real team. Nothing waits for a single distant go-live.

Built with your people

The teams who will use a screen shape it while it is being made, not during a training session afterwards.

Designed, not merely functional

Interfaces people use for eight hours a day deserve the same care as the ones customers see.

Nothing has to be migrated

Your books stay where they are. We read from the system of record and write back through it. Where a piece is replaced later, the new one runs beside the original until you have watched it work.

Documented as built

The structure, the data model and the integrations stay written down and current.

04 — Connect

Make it one system.

Screens, data and workflows joined until information moves on its own — including through the systems you chose to keep. This is the phase that turns the “before” at the top of this page into the “after”.

Systems you keep

Accounting, payroll, statutory tools and specialist machinery software are connected, not replaced.

Third parties

Payments, logistics, marketplaces, messaging, tax and compliance — connected once, in one place, so every screen can use them.

How an order crosses the line

In the system we built for PSS, a parts distributor, an order leaves the app carrying no prices. The branch ERP prices it from its own masters when the clerk opens it, and the file it receives is keyed so the same order cannot be saved twice. The ERP stays the system of record. How that was built

The manual layer disappears

The re-keying, the reconciliation, the status phone call and the daily spreadsheet export stop being work anyone has to do — so the people who were doing them go back to the parts of the job that need judgement.

If someone still carries a number from one screen to another by hand,
the connection is not finished.

05 — Evolve

Keep extending it.

The system is not handed over and left. We stay on, and new capability attaches to the structure that already holds your orders, stock and dues.

New interfaces

A new region, channel, brand or partner type gets its own screen without a new data silo behind it.

New intelligence

As the system builds up real history, more decisions become worth automating or forecasting.

Ongoing structure review

The structure is revisited as the business changes, rather than quietly drifting out of date.

Support that knows the system

The people who maintain it are the people who designed it. A piece is only replaced after you have watched the new one running.

Three things we will not do.

01

No rip-and-replace mandate

Systems that still serve the business stay, and are connected instead of discarded.

02

No one-size-fits-all ERP

The structure follows your business. Your business does not reshape itself around a product roadmap.

03

No AI for the sake of AI

Intelligence goes where it changes a decision, and nowhere else.
