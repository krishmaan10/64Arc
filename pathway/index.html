        A SHARED AGREEMENT

        Help that leaves the thinking to you.

        Know what support looks like, before you ask.

        ✓

        Support you can use

        • Understand what a task requires.
        • Break the work into manageable steps.
        • Explore questions that test your understanding.
        • Review small corrections to your own saved writing.
        • Practise adjusting tone or paraphrasing yourself.
        • Find and evaluate the curated references.
        ↳

        Work that stays with you

        • Writing essays, answers, or projects from scratch.
        • Adding arguments or paragraphs to your submission.
        • Solving an assessed exercise for you.
        • Turning someone else’s writing into your own.
        • Changing teacher permissions through a request.

        If a request crosses a boundary, the assistant explains why and suggests a permitted next step.

        What is shared?

        Saved drafts, assistance requests, replies shown, writing reviews, accepted or rejected corrections, and permission changes appear in activity. So does how each request was read, and what a teacher would notice about the sequence. Library bookmarks and task checks stay in this session too.

        What this pilot can promise

        There is no connected generative model in this preview. Guidance is fixed, and grammar replacements come from local rules. The preview cannot generate a finished project.

        The local request reader

        Each request is read twice: by fixed word patterns, and by a small classifier trained on written examples of how students ask for help and for shortcuts. It runs where you are using it, with fixed numbers, sends nothing anywhere, and its training material and measured mistakes are in the repository. It can add a refusal, or point you to a person when a message is about you rather than the work; it can never remove a refusal the patterns made. Its reading of each request is shown in activity. No generative model is connected. Requests and the decisions made about them go into a local ledger that a background learner uses to propose a better request reader; drafts and replies do not, and no change to the reader happens without a person reviewing it.

        What it cannot establish

        This does not prove who wrote pasted text or prevent use of other apps. Teacher preview is an unauthenticated review control. Authentication, student isolation, persistent storage, and evaluated model guardrails are required before school use.
