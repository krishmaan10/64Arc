What 64ARCS has built, and where each piece stops.

One business system handles orders, dues, collections and attendance for a parts distributor with 93 branches. Its field app, PSS GO, has been on the Play Store as a production app since 14 July 2026. After it come four common kinds of business, described the way we would approach them; none of those four has been built. The page ends with our two products, both early browser pilots.

PSSAO, PSS GO and PSS ONE, built for a parts distributor with 93 branches.

The distributor is Partap Singh & Sons, which sells commercial-vehicle parts across India. It is the founder’s family business, so the system was tried on a real operation with real money before it was offered to anyone else. PSSAO and PSS GO are live. PSS ONE, the ERP rebuild, is a pilot on one terminal; since 28 August 2026 it has read one branch’s real books, read-only.

There is no screenshot of the PSS ONE terminal here; it reads one branch’s real books, and we do not publish them.

PSS · Commercial-vehicle parts distribution · India

A salesman standing in a dealer’s shop could not see what that dealer owed.

Before

The business ran, and still runs, on an ERP written in VB.NET twenty years ago and installed on desktop PCs inside each of the 93 branches. A rep in a dealer’s shop carried the dues in his head, phoned the branch to ask about stock and wrote orders on paper. Attendance was a paper register, and tour expense claims were on paper too. He had no way to see whether head office had approved his branch’s credit request.

Architecture

The ERP stays, and it still holds the invoices, receipts and ledgers. PSS GO reads one copy of its data for all 93 branches, refreshed on a schedule, with dealer figures every two hours. A separate store takes what the ERP has no place for: field visits, orders, attendance and head-office decisions. The app does not post to the books. A collection recorded in it is still entered as a receipt in the ERP by a person.

Platforms

  • A field app for reps and branch staff, on Android and as an installable web app, in 13 languages
  • A dealer portal showing one balance across every branch the dealer buys from, with ordering at the catalogue price
  • A supplier portal where each of 13 brands sees its own stock by branch, valued at purchase cost, without per-dealer figures or the distributor’s margin
  • A head-office desk that decides ten kinds of branch request, from credit limits and write-offs to tour claims and leave
  • PSS ONE, the ERP rebuilt, running beside the original on one terminal as a read-only pilot

Intelligence

The collection list is ordered by a score built from the size of the dues and how late they are, raised when the dealer is over limit or has recently bounced a cheque. Before an order is written, the server checks the dealer’s credit. Over limit, ninety days overdue or a recent bounced cheque puts the order on hold; the rep can confirm past the hold, and that is recorded on the order. The same check shows a dealer who is overdue at another branch, which a rep could not see before. A spoken order, including one in mixed Hindi and English, or a photograph of a handwritten slip is turned into order lines by a language model and matched against the stock catalogue.

Outcome

A rep in a dealer’s shop now sees what the dealer owes, which branches hold the part, and where the branch’s credit request stands with head office. An order taken there reaches the dealer’s branch as an email with a file attached. The clerk double-clicks the file and it opens in the ERP as an ordinary sales order, priced by the branch’s own masters, and the ERP refuses the same file a second time. A payment cannot be recorded without its proof photograph; the two are saved in one commit. PSS ONE has not replaced the ERP anywhere. It signs in to the branch database with a read-only login, so the database refuses any write from it. It has never written a real branch’s books.

How we would approach four other kinds of business.

These four are not work we have done. Each is a common kind of business, written out under the same five headings so you can check whether yours looks like one of them. The system above is the only one on this page that exists.

Choose the one that looks most like your business.

If you are an industrial distributor

A dealer network running on phone calls, spreadsheets and an ERP nobody outside the office can reach.

Before

Dealers order by phone and message. The sales desk re-keys each order, calls the warehouse about stock and asks accounts about credit. Stock lives in a spreadsheet updated each evening, and it takes three calls to find out where an order is.

Architecture

Product, price, stock and credit would each be defined once, at the centre. The ERP would keep operations, and every other screen would read from one shared layer over it and keep no copy of its own.

Platforms

  • Dealer ordering app with contract pricing and live credit position
  • Warehouse picking and dispatch interface
  • Sales desk console for exceptions and approvals
  • Management view of channel performance

Intelligence

Replenishment suggestions for each dealer, drawn from its own buying rhythm. Credit-risk flags raised while an order is still waiting to be accepted.

What we would expect

The phone call asking where an order is would be the first thing to go. Orders would arrive structured and priced. Stock would be one number everyone shares. The sales desk would move from re-keying to the exceptions that need a person’s judgement.

If you are a manufacturer

Production planned from experience, because no system can see demand and capacity at the same time.

Before

Orders sit in one system, production is tracked on paper and whiteboards, and purchasing runs on memory. Shortages are discovered on the line. Planning depends on the two people who have done it for years.

Architecture

Demand, capacity, materials and stock would be modelled once, so a change in any of them shows up in all of them. Purchasing would be triggered by the plan.

Platforms

  • Planning board showing demand against capacity
  • Shop-floor application for job status and consumption
  • Procurement workflow driven by material shortfall
  • Quality capture attached to batch and job

Intelligence

Demand forecasts from real order history. Early warning on the items that will run short, given with enough lead time to act on it.

What we would expect

Planning would stop depending on two people’s memories. Shortages would surface weeks before they reach the machine, and what those two people know would be written down where the whole team can use it.

If you sell through more than one channel

An online store that cannot say for certain whether an item is in stock.

Before

The website holds its own catalogue and its own stock figure, synced overnight. The stores have a third view. Orders are cancelled after being accepted, and the same product is described three different ways.

Architecture

One product record and one live stock position would serve every channel. Rules set in advance would decide where each order is fulfilled from.

Platforms

  • Storefront reading live availability
  • Store application for stock, transfers and click-and-collect
  • Fulfilment console with routing rules
  • Customer account with orders, returns and history

Intelligence

Recommendations from purchase history across channels. Proposals to move stock between locations before an item sells out in one of them.

What we would expect

Every channel would tell the customer the same thing. Cancellations caused by stock that was never there would stop, and one catalogue change would update every channel.

If you run a field service operation

Engineers dispatched by phone, with the customer’s history sitting in a filing cabinet.

Before

Complaints arrive by phone and are written down. Engineers are assigned by whoever is free. Nobody knows whether the spare is available until the engineer arrives, and contract entitlements are checked by hand, sometimes after the work is done.

Architecture

The installed base, contract terms, service history and spares stock would be modelled as one record, so a ticket knows what the customer is entitled to before anyone travels.

Platforms

  • Customer service-request portal
  • Engineer field application, usable offline
  • Dispatch and scheduling console
  • Spares stock across vans and depots

Intelligence

Tickets flagged before they breach a commitment. The likely part identified from the symptom and the service history, so the van is loaded correctly the first time.

What we would expect

There would be fewer second visits. Entitlement would be known before dispatch, the history would travel with the engineer, and the customer could see where the request stands.
