64ARCS is a small software studio in India.

It builds business systems for mid-sized companies, covering orders, stock, dues and collections across their branches, and it builds products of its own.

The studio has built one business system and two products of its own.

Each is listed here with its status on 25 September 2026.

A business system for a parts distributor

In daily use since July 2026 It was built first for the founder’s own family business, Partap Singh & Sons, a parts distributor with 93 branches, where it could be tried on real orders and real money before being offered to anyone else. Its field app puts dues, stock and orders on the reps’ phones, and head office decides credit requests in it.

Pilot A rebuild of the branch ERP runs on one terminal and has read one branch’s real books, read-only, since 28 August 2026. It has never written a real branch’s books.

64Griddle, a prompt checker

Pilot It reads a prompt in the browser and suggests edits before the prompt goes to an AI agent. It uses local rules, not a model. The prompt is never uploaded. The browser pilot is at version 0.5.3, its guidance was reviewed on 8 September 2026, and the checks have not been evaluated for whether they improve an agent’s answers.

PathWay AI, a practice space for school assignments

Pilot A teacher sets the help allowed on each assignment, and the student works within it. Replies are fixed and practice questions are written in advance; no AI model is connected for replies. Its written practice questions went live on 24 September 2026. It keeps work in the browser by default and has no school accounts, so it is not ready for a school to use.

Each product has a panel on the Products page saying where its pilot stops.

How the work is done

The five phases of an engagement are set out on How It Works.

The system is built to how the business runs.

The mapping happens in the branches, watching how the work is done there. The processes and relationships a business has built up are usually why it has done well, so the system keeps them.

Decisions are explained in plain words.

A decision is treated as unfinished until it can be put in words the owner would repeat to their own team.

What already works is kept.

Where the standard way of doing something fits the business, it stays. If a spreadsheet is already doing the job, the owner hears so early.

Pieces are replaced one at a time.

The business keeps trading while the system is built. A piece is replaced only after the people who use it have watched the new one run beside the original.

Where the name comes from

The 64 refers to 64-bit computing and also has a personal meaning for the founder. An arc is a structure that carries weight while leaving an opening beneath it.

The studio is based in India and builds for businesses wherever they operate.

The businesses it builds for are mid-sized. They have outgrown disconnected software and coordination by phone and spreadsheet, and they do not want to build a large technology department of their own. A generic product usually covers most of what such a business does, and the rest is done by hand.
