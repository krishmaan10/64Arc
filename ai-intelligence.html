Where AI fits in a business system.

A model can only answer from what it can reach when it is asked, such as the orders, the stock, the dues, and who bought what and when. This page lists six jobs that a model or a plain rule already does in one working system, each with the date it went live. After those come five questions that connected records could answer, marked illustrative, and the rules every model in these systems works under.

The same question gets a different answer depending on which records the model can reach.

Connecting a language model to a website is routine work. Whether the result helps a business depends on what the model can read when a question arrives. The two panels put one question to a model in each position. The right-hand panel shows the shape of an answer, and no live system produced it.

A model on disconnected software

No context

Which customers are likely to need replenishment next week?

It can reach the page it is embedded in, and it can summarise a document pasted into it. It cannot see order history, stock, or who has stopped buying, because nothing connected it to them.

The answer is a paragraph about replenishment in general.

The reply can read fluently and still say nothing about this business, because the model reached none of its records.

A model on connected records

Full context

Which customers are likely to need replenishment next week?

It reaches order history, current stock, each customer’s buying rhythm, open orders and credit position, because those sit in one place with one definition of a customer.

The answer is a named list of accounts, with quantities and the reason each one is on it.

The question matches the left panel, and only the records underneath it differ.

Where a model, or a plain rule, already does a job.

The six examples below come from one system, the field, ordering and head-office system built for Partap Singh & Sons, a parts distributor. Four of the jobs use a language model and two run on plain rules. Each card says which, and when it went live.

Rule Live since July 2026

The collection list, ordered by what is at stake

Each morning a rep sees the accounts to chase, ordered by a score: the overdue amount, weighted by how late it is, and again if the account is over its limit or a cheque bounced recently. Whatever the rep touches today drops off the list. No model is involved; it is arithmetic, and it is the list they work from.

Model Live since July 2026

An order spoken, or photographed off a slip

A rep can dictate an order in mixed Hindi and English, or photograph a handwritten slip, and a model turns it into named parts and quantities. That only fills the cart; placing the order is the same steps as for a typed one, and the photograph stays attached. Without a model, a rule-based parser reads the spoken words instead.

Rule Live since July 2026

The credit check before an order reaches the branch

Over the limit, a cheque bounced in the last 120 days, or dues 90 days or more overdue puts the account on hold. It is a soft hold. A rep or manager can confirm through it, and the reasons then stay on the order for good. A customer ordering for themselves cannot clear it. The same check shows the rep when the account is overdue at another branch, which the field could never see before.

Model Live since July 2026

A short brief on each account

When a rep opens an account, a model writes a few plain lines on where it stands, in English, Hindi or Marathi. Without a model, a fixed template fills the same space.

Model Live since 2 July 2026

A second opinion on a sanction request

When a branch asks head office to raise a credit limit, the request arrives with the customer’s identity, the branch’s ask, a rule-based risk read and, where switched on, a model’s recommendation. The model only recommends. The rule-based read does more. It marks files that sit inside a band backtested on past approvals as clean, and head office can approve all of those in one tap over its choice. A scheduled path can also approve them unattended, under a rupee cap; it is off by default. A sample of its approvals is queued for a person to re-check. Everything else is decided by a person, who can approve, reject, or set a different number.

Model Live since July 2026

A question to the books, in plain words

The owner can ask the live ERP a question in plain language and get an answer from the records, read-only. If the answer calls for a change, the model can only propose the exact statement; the owner confirms it, and the write is gated at the database as well as in the app.

Questions that connected records could answer, and the shape of each answer.

None of the five below runs in a system built here today. Each shows what could come back once orders, stock, customers and sales history are linked. Each would be built separately, after that linking is done.

Which customers are likely to need replenishment next week?

A named list of accounts, ranked by likely order value, each with the reason it is on the list.

Why did margin decline last month?

The lines where freight recovery fell, beside the dealers whose discounts rose.

Which open service tickets are at risk of breaching commitment?

The tickets at risk, each with what it is waiting on, whether an engineer or a part that has yet to be ordered.

What should we manufacture next month?

A proposed plan, with the items that run short if nothing changes.

What is our warranty position on this model sold through a dealer in 2023?

The applicable clause, the covered period, and what has already been claimed.

All five are illustrative: the shape of the answer, not output from a live system.

The ways a model can sit on connected records, and which of them runs today.

One of these runs today, for two roles, read-only. The rest are designed and not built. Before you commit to any of it, you will know which parts exist and which would be new work for your business.

Assistants

Plain-language access to the business, scoped by role: a manager asks about their branches, a dealer about their account, an owner about the company, and each gets an answer bounded by what they are allowed to see. Today this runs for two roles, both read-only: head-office managers asking questions over a bounded snapshot of receivables, stock and branches, and the owner asking the live ERP. Assistants for the other roles are designed and not built yet.

Agents

Software that carries out a defined task end to end: chasing an overdue confirmation, preparing a replenishment proposal, checking an order against contract terms. None of these is built yet. The sanction engine is the one part that runs today and decides on its own. It marks credit files inside a backtested band as clean, and a person’s tap or a capped scheduled run approves them, with a sample queued for a person to re-check. Where a language model can propose a change to the books, a person confirms it. Any agent built later would start from those two rules.

Prediction

Demand, replenishment, stock cover, cash position and risk, each forecast from the company’s own transaction history. Not built yet.

Knowledge

Policies, specifications, contracts and precedent, made answerable, so what one person knows does not leave with them. Not built yet.

The rules every model in these systems works under.

A model is added only where it changes a decision. Two of the six jobs above use no model at all and run on plain rules.

Permissions apply to a model as they apply to people. The owner’s assistant reads the books through a read-only login; it has no other way in.

Where a decision carries real consequence, a person decides or checks. A hold is confirmed by a rep or manager; a sanction outside the engine’s backtested band by head office, and a sample of the ones inside it is queued for a person to re-check; a write to the books by the owner.

What goes through leaves its reasons on the record. An order confirmed past a hold carries them for good.

Take the model away and the system still runs. Every model is optional; each feature falls back to a rule or says it is unavailable.
