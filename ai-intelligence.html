Put AI to work on a useful task.

For a team working from paper, phone calls and established software, AI may help read an order, summarise an account or find information in records. Start with the effort the task takes today, then include the time needed to check the result. A model belongs only where that leaves the team with an easier job.

Fit the help into the work already happening.

Choose a task with a result someone can check. Keep the useful tools around it, and compare the whole job before and after the change.

Reduce the steps staff take

A suggestion should appear where someone needs it. If using AI means copying records into another tool and bringing the answer back, count those extra steps against any time saved.

Choose a rule when it is enough

Totals, credit limits and fixed checks belong in ordinary software. A model is more useful for varied wording or documents, where its interpretation can be reviewed.

Try it with real work

Agree what a useful result looks like, then check typical cases and mistakes with staff. Keep a way to complete the task when the suggestion is wrong or the model is unavailable.

Less typing, less reading and clearer follow-ups.

These examples come from a customer application in daily use since July 2026. They show where a model helps with varied input and where ordinary software is enough. The distributor case study describes the implementation.

Model Live since July 2026

Read an order from a slip

A photograph of a handwritten order can fill a cart with parts and quantities. Staff can also dictate in mixed Hindi and English. The result still goes through the usual order steps, and the photograph stays attached for checking.

Model Live since July 2026

Read a short account summary

Opening an account brings up a few lines about its position in English, Hindi or Marathi. A fixed template fills the same space when the model is unavailable, so the summary has a fallback within the existing screen.

Rule Live since July 2026

Put overdue accounts in order

A collection list ranks accounts using overdue amounts, how late they are, credit limits and recent cheque returns. Accounts touched that day leave the list. This is a calculation with defined inputs; it needs no language model.

Give the task the records it needs.

An account summary needs the right account records. Reading an order needs the slip and enough product information to check the items. Neither is a reason to rebuild every tool the business uses.

Keep the source visible

Keep the document or records beside the suggestion so someone can check names, quantities and dates. Treat missing or old information as a reason to check further.

Limit what the model can reach

Agree which records the task needs and who may see the result. Connect that access through the tools the team uses, with permissions that match the person doing the work.

Keep changes under control

Reading records and changing them need separate decisions. Set the approval steps before adding a model, including who can confirm a proposed change and how it is recorded.

Keep AI only when the whole task gets easier.

Count checking and correction

Compare time spent from the original input to a checked result. Include correcting mistakes, moving between screens and teaching staff how to use the change.

Try the awkward cases

Use unclear handwriting, incomplete records and unfamiliar wording in the trial. Agree when staff should correct a suggestion or complete the task by hand.

Keep a usable fallback

Decide how work continues during an outage and what the ongoing cost will be. If a rule or a simpler process does the job with less effort, choose that.

PathWay AI

Students work inside the boundary their teacher sets for each project, and each session leaves a record of how the work happened. The record makes no claim about who wrote it. In the preview, guidance is fixed and grammar corrections come from local rules.

64Griddle

It reads a prompt to an AI assistant before you send it, marks where the assistant would have to guess, and offers wording you accept or reject. Nothing in your draft changes unless you choose it. It uses local rules, not a model. It reads only the textbox in front of you.
