Where AI fits

AI becomes useful when it understands the business.

A chatbot on top of software that does not talk to itself is still just a chatbot. A model is useful for what it can reach when it is asked: the orders, the stock, the dues, who bought what and when. Below: what already runs, what connected records make possible, and the rules we hold to either way.

The difference

The model is not the hard part.
The context is.

Anyone can connect a language model to a website. Whether it is any use in a business comes down to a different question: when it is asked something, what can it actually reach? The right-hand panel is the shape of the answer, not a screen we can show you today.

A model on disconnected software

No context

Which customers are likely to need replenishment next week?

It can reach the page it is embedded in. It can summarise a document you paste. It cannot see your order history, your stock, or who has stopped buying — because nothing connected it to them.

The answer is a paragraph about replenishment in general.

Confident. Fluent. Useless.

A model on connected records

Full context

Which customers are likely to need replenishment next week?

It reaches order history, current stock, each customer’s buying rhythm, open orders and credit position — because those sit in one place, with one definition of a customer.

The answer is a named list of accounts, with quantities and the reason each one is on it.

The same question. Different records underneath.

What runs today

Where a model, or a plain rule, already does a job.

The real examples on this page come from one system: the field, ordering and head-office system we built for Partap Singh & Sons, a parts distributor. Not all of it is a language model. Some of it is arithmetic, and each card says which.

Rule Live

The collection list, ordered by what is at stake

Each morning a rep sees the accounts to chase, ordered by a score: the overdue amount, weighted by how late it is, and again if the account is over its limit or a cheque bounced recently. Whatever the rep touches today drops off the list. No model is involved; it is arithmetic, and it is the list they work from.

Model Live

An order spoken, or photographed off a slip

A rep can dictate an order in mixed Hindi and English, or photograph a handwritten slip, and a model turns it into named parts and quantities. That only fills the cart; placing the order is the same steps as for a typed one, and the photograph stays attached. Without a model, a rule-based parser reads the spoken words instead.

Rule Live

The credit check before an order reaches the branch

Over the limit, a cheque bounced in the last 120 days, or dues 90 days or more overdue puts the account on hold. It is a soft hold: a rep or manager can confirm through it, and the reasons then stay on the order for good. A customer ordering for themselves cannot clear it. The same check shows the rep when the account is overdue at another branch, which the field could never see before.

Model Live

A short brief on each account

When a rep opens an account, a model writes a few plain lines on where it stands, in English, Hindi or Marathi. Without a model, a fixed template fills the same space.

Model Live

A second opinion on a sanction request

When a branch asks head office to raise a credit limit, the request arrives with the customer’s identity, the branch’s ask, a rule-based risk read and, where switched on, a model’s recommendation. The model only recommends. The rule-based read does more: it marks files that sit inside a band backtested on past approvals as clean, and head office can approve all of those in one tap over its choice. A scheduled path can also approve them unattended, under a rupee cap; it is off by default. A sample of the machine’s calls is re-checked by a person. Everything else, a person decides — approve, reject, or a different number.

Model Live

A question to the books, in plain words

The owner can ask the live ERP a question in plain language and get an answer from the records, read-only. If the answer calls for a change, the model can only propose the exact statement; the owner confirms it, and the write is gated at the database as well as in the app.

The rule underneath

Every model here is optional.
Take it away and the system still runs: each feature falls back to a rule, or says it is unavailable.

What connected records make possible

Five questions, and the shape of the answer.

We do not run any of these five. They show what the same kind of question comes back with once orders, stock, customers and sales history share one definition of a customer. Each is marked illustrative; each is something we would build towards one at a time, after the records are connected.

MarginIllustrative Why did margin decline in the West region last month?

Result The lines where freight recovery fell, beside the dealers whose discounts rose.
ServiceIllustrative Which open service tickets are at risk of breaching commitment?

All five illustrative — the shape of the answer, not output from a live system.

The order of work

Connect the records first.
The model is the easy part after that.

Four forms

Four ways a model can sit on connected records.

One of these runs today, for two roles, read-only. The rest are designed and not built. We will tell you which is which for your business before you commit to any of it.

Assistants

Plain-language access to the business, scoped by role: a manager asks about their branches, a dealer about their account, an owner about the company, and each gets an answer bounded by what they are allowed to see. Today this runs for two roles, both read-only: head-office managers asking questions over a bounded snapshot of receivables, stock and branches, and the owner asking the live ERP. The rest is designed, not built.

Agents

Software that carries out a defined task end to end: chasing an overdue confirmation, preparing a replenishment proposal, checking an order against contract terms. Not built as such. One thing we run does decide on its own: the sanction engine marks credit files inside a proven band as clean, and a person’s tap or a capped scheduled run approves them, with a sample re-checked by a person. Where a language model can propose a change to the books, a person confirms it. Any agent we build starts from those two rules.

Prediction

Demand, replenishment, stock cover, cash position and risk, built on your own transaction history rather than a generic model of an industry. Not built yet.

Knowledge

Policies, specifications, contracts and precedent, made answerable, so what one person knows does not leave with them. Not built yet.

Guardrails

Useful.
Not AI theatre.

A model goes in where it changes a decision — not where it makes a screenshot look modern.

Permissions apply to a model as they apply to people. The owner’s assistant reads the books through a read-only login; it has no other way in.

Where a decision carries real consequence, a person decides or checks. A hold is confirmed by a rep or manager; a sanction outside the engine’s proven band by head office, and a sample of the ones inside it is re-checked by a person; a write to the books by the owner.

What goes through leaves its reasons on the record. An order confirmed past a hold carries them for good.

Take the model away and the system still runs. Every model is optional; each feature falls back to a rule or says it is unavailable.
