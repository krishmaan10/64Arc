Make everyday work easier to get done.

Start with a task people repeat

Watch where someone copies a message into a form, reads a paper order or phones for a figure. Choose a task that takes time every day and agree what a useful improvement would look like.

Choose a frequent task with a clear input and an outcome staff can check. Record how long it takes today, including corrections, so there is a fair comparison with the proposed change.

Trace inputs, outputs and exceptions through one workflow. Establish a baseline for completion time and errors, and test on representative examples before choosing an implementation.

Use AI where it earns its place

AI can help turn spoken or handwritten orders into items someone can check, or put account details into plain words. A calculation or a fixed business rule may need no AI at all.

Consider a model for language or varied documents. Keep calculations and fixed checks in ordinary software. Include review time, running cost and mistakes when deciding whether a model helps.

Use a model for extraction or summarisation where inputs vary. Keep arithmetic and policy checks deterministic. Compare the full workflow with and without the model, including validation failures and latency.

Keep the work familiar

Build around the screens, records and habits that still serve the business. The aim is fewer steps for the person doing the job, with a clear way to check and correct a suggestion.

Put assistance at the point where the task happens. Avoid a separate tool that asks staff to copy information between systems. Keep the existing source for prices, balances and other agreed facts.

Connect through the existing system’s supported interfaces and enforce permissions on the server. Keep authoritative values in their owning system and treat generated content as a proposal to validate.

Make it useful without the model

A person needs a way to finish the task when AI gets something wrong or is unavailable. Decide what needs checking, who can approve a change and how to carry on without the suggestion.

Agree which actions need confirmation and how staff recover from an error. Try the change with the people who will use it, and include a manual route or a rule-based fallback where needed.

Define failure behaviour for missing records, invalid output and model outages. Test permission boundaries and write validation separately from generation, with an explicit fallback for the workflow.

A simpler form, a connected record or a clearer process may be enough. The choice depends on the task and the people doing it.
